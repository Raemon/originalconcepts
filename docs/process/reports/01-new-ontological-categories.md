## Ontological invention: findings

My top 3 turned out to be one family. Each takes one way the cause-to-effect relation can be defective and turns it into a kind of particle:
- **Flukes:** a correlation with no cause.
- **Spares:** a cause with no effect.
- **Almosts:** an effect whose cause fell just short.

None of the three appears in my searches as a game mechanic. All three have real precedents as concepts, listed below.

### #1 FLUKES (correlation without cause)
**Concept:** A coincidence is a real entity. It is born wherever two things line up (same cell, matching edge colour, same beat) and the properties that line up have completely separate causal histories. If any single event is an ancestor of both, the alignment is "explained" and nothing is born.
**Logic:**
- Every property carries a lineage: the set of independent sources that determined it.
- Relative changes (push, roll) add to a lineage. Absolute settings (a reset, a hidden shuffler) replace it.
- Seeing is a causal channel. Looking at B pours B's lineage into you, and anything you touch afterwards inherits it.

**Consequences:**
- You can author at most one half of any coincidence.
- Looking at a target spoils your shot at it.
- Knowing the *laws* doesn't contaminate you; only knowing *facts* does. So you can aim blind by pure deduction.
- The only way to clean something is an absolute reset.

**New mental operation:** Causal hygiene. You plan what you must *not* know, and treat your own eyes as a wire in the circuit that you have to cut yourself out of.
**Precedents (honest, about 55%):** Cournot's definition of chance as the meeting of independent causal chains (1843, defended by Monod). Reichenbach's common-cause principle. Jung's synchronicity. Double-blind and randomised-trial design. Adams' Improbability Drive. Melas & Salis' "hybrid" account even includes the coincidence-spotter's "access to information". What I believe is new: luck as a substance you can harvest only by engineering your own ignorance. Searches turned up no game like this.
**Puzzles:**
1. A luck-socket creeps through a dark room by a law printed on the door. Opening the shutter to look would contaminate you, so you count ticks, close your eyes and release your ball.
2. You glanced at a beetle and are now contaminated. You let it wander through a hidden shuffler, whose absolute reset wipes its position lineage, and it is a stranger again.
3. Two crates you've both pushed can never coincide in a way that counts. An untouched drifting buoy has to carry the alignment instead.

**Visual:** Each independent causal source has a hue, and objects show their ancestry as bands of colour. Your sightlines act like straws that tint you. Coincidences flare only where two palettes share no band.

### #2 SPARES (cause without effect)
**Concept:** When a state that is already caused gets another sufficient cause, the redundant cause's power doesn't vanish. It breaks off as a Spare: a free unit of causation with no effect and no origin.
**Logic:**
- Each effect has one owning cause, whichever arrives first.
- A later sufficient cause is pre-empted. It mints a Spare and then goes hollow: it is still physically there but no longer holds the effect.
- Simultaneous causes share ownership and mint nothing.
- Spending a Spare creates an *uncaused* state. It never reverts, because there is no cause to withdraw. Only an opposing mechanism can undo it.

**Consequences:**
- Redundancy is a mine you can dig.
- Spending the redundancy removes it. If the owner steps off, the door shuts even though the backup crate is still sitting on its plate.
- The order in which causes arrive matters.

**New mental operation:** Counting the sufficient causes behind every effect, and treating "already true" as an opportunity rather than waste.
**Precedents (about 40%):** Lewis on late pre-emption ("spoils to the victor"), the "trumping" literature, Kim's exclusion argument, and Ferey & Dehez's Shapley-valued "overdetermined causation games". Searches found no game or fiction that turns the pre-empted cause into a detachable thing.
**Puzzles:**
1. Two plates both open Door A, and Door B has no mechanism at all. Stand on plate 1, shove a crate onto plate 2, and the "useless" second button pops the key.
2. A gate has to stay open after you leave, so you open it uncaused.
3. A Spare drifting along wires will set off a mine. You get two causes onto the same tick so no Spare is minted.

**Visual:** Every state hangs from visible "because" tethers. Pre-empted tethers snap and curl into glowing knots. Uncaused states float with no tether at all.

### #3 ALMOSTS (effect without sufficient cause)
**Concept:** When a trigger condition is missed by exactly one unit on any dimension (one cell, one tick, one unit of weight), a fraction of that event is deposited where it would have happened. N *distinct* near-misses add up to the event actually happening, with N printed on each device.
**Logic:**
- Near-misses only count if they are different: different objects, direction or dimension. Repeating the same one does nothing.
- If the event really happens, its stored near-misses are cleared.

**New mental operation:** Aiming to fail by exactly one, listing the distinct ways you could nearly succeed, and mapping how close the current state is to each event.
**Precedents (about 60%):** Gerstenberg & Tenenbaum (2016) define "almost" in exactly this way: a small perturbation would have been enough. Also Touhou's "graze" scoring, Burnout's near-miss boost, research on near-misses in gambling, and the "near-miss indicator" design pattern. What I found nowhere is near-misses adding up to the event itself.
**Puzzles:**
1. A plate needs weight 4, and the level's crates weigh 1, 1 and 2. Find three distinct ways to put weight 3 on it.
2. A sealed sensor can't be reached, so you graze it from three angles.
3. A turret fires one tick after you pass. Survive three near-misses and the warden's "prisoner died" door opens.

**Visual:** Like an animator's pencil test. Events are sketched in progressively firmer strokes until they are inked in, and off-by-one gaps get a hairline caret.

### Other candidates (brief)
4. **Debts:** Every push creates an IOU: the pushed thing owes the pusher one push, paid back automatically the first time they are adjacent. IOUs can be passed on, and they cancel out around cycles. *Mental operation:* bookkeeping as movement. *Precedents:* Newton's third law with a delay, multilateral netting (about 55%). *Puzzle:* bank a boulder's debt and cash it at a chasm edge to get shoved across. *Visual:* ledger threads with knots.
5. **Culpa:** Every outcome gives off blame, shared among its causes by degree of responsibility (1/(k+1)). Relics shatter when blamed and idols wake at 1.0. *Mental operation:* working out which cause was pivotal, and diluting blame with redundancy. *Precedents:* Chockler–Halpern responsibility, Shapley values, Jain karma as literal matter (about 65%).
6. **Curie Locks:** Any symmetry a layout acquires becomes a being on its axis. It forces every further change to respect the symmetry until a chiral token breaks it. Standing on the axis, you can only move along it. *Mental operation:* working out the board's symmetries as you play. *Precedents:* Curie's principle, mirror-control puzzles (about 60%, close to the excluded "one input, many bodies").
7. **Equivocations:** Objects are the world's reading of coloured cells as shapes from a fixed set. Where two readings tie, an entity exists, and its properties go to whichever reading your next cell settles. *Precedents:* Quine's gavagai, Gestalt grouping, Jelly no Puzzle (about 65%).
8. **Loci:** Walls that are geometric loci, such as points equidistant from two foci, and you are one of the foci. Example: a wall that always stays between you and the lamp. *Precedents:* Euclidea, Voronoi diagrams (about 70%).
9. **Prototypes:** Every magnitude is a multiple of a physical exemplar, and each object is calibrated to a particular exemplar. Filing one exemplar down rescales everything calibrated to it. *Precedents:* Wittgenstein's standard metre, scale-shifting games (about 70%).
10. **Barycentric Fusions:** Bind scattered objects together and their centre of mass becomes a body that can press a plate in mid-air. *Precedents:* Lewis' unrestricted composition, balance puzzles (about 60%).
11. **Deadlocks:** A cycle of movers, each waiting on the next, creates an indestructible knot you can build with. Only pre-emption dissolves it. *Precedents:* concurrency theory, Sokoban deadlocks (about 65%).
12. **The Many:** Four or more alike things lose their individuality and become a pourable mass. Drain it below four and they become individuals again in a fixed order, so you lose track of who was who. *Precedents:* the count/mass distinction in linguistics, falling-sand games (about 60%).

### Combination: Flukes × Spares gives Laundered Agency ("manufactured luck")
Spares carry no lineage: they are causal power that never produced an effect, so nothing was ever stamped on them. A state set by a Spare therefore has empty ancestry. That means it can take part in a Fluke even though you arranged it.

**Example:** You have looked at the luck-socket, so any lever you pull taints the ball's release. Instead, you double up the causes on some unrelated door, carry off the Spare, and use it to open the ball's gate. The ball's path now descends from nothing you touched, so it counts as a fluke.

**The emergent third concept:** What you play with becomes *who the world credits for an event*, not the event itself. You author outcomes that are intended and yet certifiably accidental: building alibis, laundering intent. Neither parent has this on its own. Flukes alone forbid authorship, and Spares alone are just remote triggers. Almosts could join as a third thread, completing the trio of surplus, deficit and absence of cause.
